### THE FINDING OF THE WEEK: the cuff limit was a technique problem, not a capacity problem

**The rotator cuff ticked at 28 kg during a cold warmup — and was completely quiet at 36 kg once the entry changed.** That is a 29% heavier load, silent, on the same joint, in the same session.

#### What changed: the entry, not the press

**MEC identified it himself — the provocation was getting the dumbbells into position, not pressing them.** The conventional palms-forward kick-up forces the shoulder into **loaded external rotation**, which is already a named restriction in this file. The press itself was never the problem.

**Two fixes were found in-session, and both are now permanent protocol:**

| Fix | Where it works |
|---|---|
| **Neutral grip on entry** — dumbbells facing each other, rotate only once supported | **Both presses.** Keeps the shoulder packed through the entry |
| **Leg drive** — dumbbells on thighs, knees drive them up while lying back | **Flat only.** MEC confirms it does not transfer to incline, where the thighs are not under the weight |

#### The leg drive is not an energy shortcut, it removes a hidden tax

**MEC flagged honestly that the leg drive "gave some energy to make 8" on the last flat set.** That is the technique working as designed, not a confound.

**The effort previously spent muscling dumbbells into position never trained the chest.** It was taken off the top of every set. The leg drive does not inflate the reps — **it stops the entry from stealing them.** The implication is that his recorded loads have been understating him for some time.

#### The incline entry still has no leg-drive solution

**Confirmed by the athlete: the technique does not work on an incline bench.** Three options, in order of preference:

1. **Hand-off at 34 kg and above.** The one exercise in the programme where a spotter genuinely matters
2. **Drop the bench to ~30°.** Two gains at once — partial leg drive becomes usable, and less shoulder flexion is kinder to the cuff than the standard 45°
3. **Incline machine press for the top sets** if neither is available

#### And a protocol note on warming the entry itself

**The tick came at 28 kg cold. The entry at 36 kg, warm, was silent.** Working hypothesis: **the entry is not dangerous in general, it is dangerous cold.**

**Rule: never make the first incline entry at a working-relevant load. Ramp the entry deliberately** — 20 kg, then 28 kg — before anything heavy is handled. This happened by accident today and should happen on purpose next week.

**If 28 kg ticks again next week after a full ramp, the entry is a harder constraint than it looked today.**

### This reopens a much larger question for the physio

**Pre-injury flat dumbbell press was 50 kg.** Today's 40 kg is 80% of that, reached without shoulder complaint.

**Three independent findings this week now point the same way:**

1. **31 July** — reps rose across sets on three exercises: loads were too light
2. **1 August** — +4 kg on both presses in a single session, reps holding
3. **Historical capacity of 50 kg**, against a programme that has been working at 32 to 36

**The conclusion is that the programme has been under-loading him.** The caution was correct when it was set — the injury was real. **But it has been carried forward for years without ever being retested against what the shoulder can currently do.**

**The question for the physio is no longer "is my shoulder ok" but "what can I load, and what specifically still needs restricting?"** Today's evidence is that at least one restriction was really a setup problem in disguise.

## 2. THE VOLUME BROKE — and the prediction held

**Reported: "finally one big" movement, but "very soft, like swoosh."**

| Dimension | Status |
|---|---|

| **Consistency** | **Overshot.** "Very soft, like a swoosh" reads as **Bristol Type 6**, possibly 6 to 7 |

**Every single passage recorded between 27 July and 2 August was small volume with incomplete evacuation. That is nine days and roughly fifteen passages. This is the first one that was not.**

### The 2 August prediction was met, and it should be scored honestly

**The prediction, set on 2 August:**

> *If fibre holds at 21 to 25 g and water holds near 2 L, volume should improve within 24 to 48 hours.*

**Yesterday delivered ~24 to 27 g of fibre and ~1.5 L of water plus ~730 ml of other drinks. Volume improved inside 24 hours.**

**But it is not clean evidence, and the honest reading matters:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **In favour** | The timing fits the stated window precisely, and fibre and water were the only two inputs deliberately changed |
| **Against** | **A retained backlog clears eventually regardless of what is done.** Nine days of incomplete evacuation had to end at some point, and a large soft passage is the classic signature of exactly that |
| **Confounded by** | Polyols ran at ~20 g yesterday, the highest since 30 July and at the threshold where osmotic symptoms begin |

**Verdict: consistent with the hypothesis, not proof of it. The clean test is whether volume stays normal over the next two to three days while water is held.**

### The softness is expected, not a new problem

**A large, very soft passage after nine days of hard, small, incomplete ones is the textbook picture of a backlog clearing**, not of a new diarrhoeal process.

**Three plausible contributors, all benign:**

1. **The backlog itself.** Material retained in the colon for days, released at once, having been softened by whatever water finally reached it
2. **Fibre and water arriving together for the first time.** This is precisely the combination that was missing, and it is meant to produce soft, bulky, easily passed stool
3. **~20 g of polyols yesterday**, at the threshold where osmotic loosening begins in unselected adults

**No action.** One loose passage after a week of constipation is the system finding equilibrium, and Type 6 is on the correct side of the problem being treated.

### What would change that

**If stools stay at Type 6 or looser for two or more consecutive days, the plan reverses:**

| Lever | Action if looseness persists |
|---|---|
| **Bars** | First to cut. ~20 g of polyols daily is the most likely contributor |
| **Figs** | Reduce from 40 to 60 g to ~30 g |
| **Water** | **Do not reduce.** Water does not cause loose stool, and it is the intervention under test |
| Magnesium bisglycinate | Leave alone. Weak osmotic action, and it is there for sleep |

**Target state is Bristol Type 4** — formed, smooth, passed without straining. **Today is on the far side of that, which is a better place to be than where the week started.**
